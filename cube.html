Front

Vision and first impression

The entry face: what someone should understand in the first few seconds.

  • Lead with the clearest value statement
  • Show the primary user problem being solved
  • Keep the language short enough to scan while the cube is locked
  • Highlight the one action you want the viewer to take next
  • Use blue accents to signal trust and clarity
  • Pair the message with a clean, structural visual
  • Make this the default story when presenting live
  • Update this list with your product pitch bullets

Back

Challenges and reverse view

The opposite angle: friction, objections, and what sits behind the pitch.

  • Call out the main risks or blockers honestly
  • Explain what happens if the problem stays unsolved
  • List common objections and the short rebuttal for each
  • Show dependencies that are easy to overlook
  • Use red accents for urgency without sounding alarming
  • Keep one bullet for “what we will not do”
  • Add evidence or lessons learned from past attempts
  • End with the recovery path back to the front vision

Right

Growth and momentum

Forward motion: opportunities, expansion, and near-term wins.

  • List the next three growth bets in priority order
  • Define what “good” looks like in 30 / 60 / 90 days
  • Call out the audience or market segment to unlock first
  • Map the quickest path from demo to adoption
  • Use green accents to signal progress and health
  • Include one metric that proves momentum
  • Note partnerships or channels that amplify reach
  • Keep a bullet for the single biggest unlock this quarter

Left

Context and constraints

Supporting detail: environment, limits, and the frame around the decision.

  • Describe the current operating environment
  • List hard constraints (time, budget, compliance, tech)
  • Name the stakeholders who must stay aligned
  • Capture assumptions that still need validation
  • Use yellow accents for attention and caution
  • Include adjacent systems this topic depends on
  • Note what is explicitly out of scope
  • Keep a bullet for open questions to resolve next

Top

Strategy and ambition

The high-level view: direction, principles, and long-range intent.

  • State the north-star outcome in one sentence
  • List the guiding principles that shape decisions
  • Show how this face connects the other five topics
  • Define the 12-month ambition without diving into tasks
  • Use purple accents for elevation and imagination
  • Include strategic bets that require executive buy-in
  • Call out the non-negotiable quality bar
  • End with the story you want leaders to repeat

Bottom

Foundations and operations

The base layer: delivery mechanics, reliability, and what keeps things running.

  • List the core systems and owners behind the experience
  • Define the minimum operational checklist
  • Call out monitoring, support, and escalation paths
  • Document the release or rollout rhythm
  • Use orange accents for energy and practicality
  • Include cost, capacity, or reliability guardrails
  • Note the handoff points between teams
  • Keep one bullet for the next foundation improvement
